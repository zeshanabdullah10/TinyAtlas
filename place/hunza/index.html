Hunza Valley

Gilgit-Baltistan, Pakistan

Hunza was a princely state, bordering China to the north-east and Pamir to its north-west, which continued to survive until 1974, when it was finally dissolved by Zulfikar Ali Bhutto. The state bordered the Gilgit Agency to the south, the former princely state of Nagar to the east. The state capital was the town of Baltit and its old settlement is Ganish Village. Know before you go

Getting there

  • Hunza is a 100 km drive from Gilgit, and the drive takes almost 2-3 hours. Hunza Valley
  • The journey from Islamabad can take as long as 24 hours. Hunza Valley
  • From Kashgar there is a regular international bus service to Hunza via Sost, crossing the Khunjerab Pass. Hunza Valley

Getting around

  • Private vehicles are normally used as local transport. Hunza Valley
  • Renting jeeps is also a common way of moving around. Hunza Valley
  • The main bus stand is on the KKH Aliabad, with booking agents in town for long-distance buses and jeeps along the KKH. Hunza Valley

When to go

  • The Khunjerab Pass is open from May 1st to Dec 30th, but closed in winter. Hunza Valley
  • Rakaposhi's climate is generally favorable, with long periods of exceptional weather not uncommon throughout the summer. Hunza Valley
  • Flights to Gilgit are subject to weather clearance, and in winters flights are often delayed by several days. Hunza Valley

Permits and fees

  • From Tashkurgan, you can go to immigration to get a departure approval before continuing toward Sost. Hunza Valley

Altitude and health

  • The Khunjerab Pass is about 5000 m high. Hunza Valley

Local customs

  • Most of the people of Hunza are Islmaili Muslims, followers of the Aga Khan. Hunza Valley
