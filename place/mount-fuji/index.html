Mount Fuji

Shizuoka Prefecture, Japan

Mount Fuji is an active stratovolcano located on the Japanese island of Honshu, with a summit elevation of 3,776.24 m. It is the highest mountain in Japan, the second-highest volcano on any Asian island, and the seventh-highest peak of an island on Earth. Mount Fuji last erupted from 1707 to 1708. Know before you go

Getting there

  • From Tokyo, the easiest and most popular option is a direct bus from Shinjuku to the trailhead at Kawaguchiko Fifth Station. Mount Fuji
  • The Keio/Fujikyu Express Bus runs from Shinjuku Expressway Bus Terminal to Lake Kawaguchi; separate buses go to Kawaguchiko 5th station and do not run if snow blocks the route. Mount Fuji
  • During warm months, buses run from Shinjuku directly to the 5th station. Mount Fuji

Getting around

  • Once on the mountain, the only way of getting around is on foot. Mount Fuji
  • Horseback riding is available on the Fujiguchiko trail between the 5th and 7th stations only, for ¥14,000. Mount Fuji

When to go

  • Official climbing season lasts from early July to early September, with exact dates varying by year and trail. Mount Fuji
  • Even during climbing season, temperatures at the top can be below freezing at night, so climbers must dress adequately. Mount Fuji
  • Climbing outside the official season is extremely dangerous without alpine climbing experience and equipment; nearly all facilities are closed and all roads to the 5th station are shut out of season. Mount Fuji

Permits and fees

  • Outside the official season, climbers are required to submit a climbing plan to the Yamanashi Prefectural Police or Shizuoka Prefectural Police; this has been mandatory since 2019. Mount Fuji

Money and connectivity

  • Buses from Shinjuku to Lake Kawaguchi cost ¥1750; separate buses to Kawaguchiko 5th station cost ¥1540 one way or ¥2100 round trip. Mount Fuji
  • Tolls from inner Tokyo via the Chuo Expressway from Shinjuku are around ¥2500. Mount Fuji

Staying safe

  • Climbing outside the official season is extremely dangerous without alpine climbing experience and equipment; winter weather is vicious and people have been blown off the mountain by high winds. Mount Fuji
